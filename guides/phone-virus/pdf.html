Nuke (almost) any virus
off your phone.

Do the steps in order and stop as soon as the problem is gone.

Before you start: if you think a partner or an ex put stalkerware on your phone, removing it can alert them and delete evidence. Get help and make a safety plan first, from a different device: stopstalkerware.org.

1

Find the bad app 5 minutes

Android: restart in Safe Mode. If the pop-ups stop, an app is the problem, so uninstall your newest apps one by one. If one won't uninstall, go to Settings → Security → Device admin apps, remove it there, then uninstall it. Then open Accessibility and turn off anything you don't recognize.

iPhone: delete calendar subscriptions you didn't add, and remove any profile you didn't install in Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.

Both: block notifications from websites you don't know.

Still acting weird? Go to step 2.

2

Scan it and check your accounts

Android: run a Play Protect scan, then a free scan with Malwarebytes. iPhone: skip antivirus apps, iOS doesn't let them scan.

Worried about real spyware? iVerify Basic, or Amnesty's free Mobile Verification Toolkit.

Sign out every device you don't recognize on Google, Apple and WhatsApp (Settings → Linked devices), then change your passwords from a computer you trust.

Still infected? Step 3.

3

Factory reset, the right way

Back up your photos, contacts and files, not your apps (an app backup can bring the malware back). Reset the phone, set it up as new, install updates first, then download your apps fresh from the official store.

Came back after a reset? It's hiding in the system. Step 4.

4

Reinstall the whole system

Pixel: Android Flash Tool in Chrome (wipes everything, relock the bootloader after). Samsung: Smart Switch recovery or a service center. iPhone: recovery mode → Restore.

A cheap no-name phone with no official firmware? Honestly, replace it.

5

Maximum protection if someone is really after you

Restart your phone every week (the NSA's advice). iPhone: turn on Lockdown Mode. Android 16: turn on Advanced Protection.

A real Apple threat notification never asks you to click a link or install anything. Journalists and activists can get free help from Amnesty's Security Lab and Access Now.

What actually keeps you safe

Updates. They alone stopped the Play Store attack from the video. If your phone doesn't get them anymore, a supported phone fixes more than any app.

Buying Android? Make sure it's Play Protect certified, and skip weirdly cheap no-name boxes (FBI warning).

"Your phone has 13 viruses" pop-ups are ads. The cleaner app they push is the actual risk.
